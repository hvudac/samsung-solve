# Samsung Solve for Tomorrow 2026: competition brief

Extracted on 23 September 2026 from the three public sources below. Where the sources
disagree or say nothing, the point is marked **Open** instead of guessed. When the
organiser publishes more (registration form, deliverable briefs, judging criteria),
save it verbatim next to this file with its date and update this brief.

**Sources**
- Samsung NL page: https://www.samsung.com/nl/explore/brand/solve-for-tomorrow/
- Soapbox challenge page, the registration platform (steponthebox.com/challenges redirects to Soapbox): https://soapbox.nl/challenges/challenge-yHRcBY86HvIM/
- Samsung Newsroom NL, launch article of 12 June 2026: https://news.samsung.com/nl/samsung-electronics-benelux-opent-jacht-op-de-beste-studenten-ideeen-voor-solve-for-tomorrow-2026

## Who can enter

- Students aged **16 to 25** (Newsroom and Soapbox).
- Benelux: Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg.
- Teams of **2 to 5** people, assembled by the students themselves. We are 3.
- **Open:** whether teammates must be at the same school, any education-level limit, the language of the deliverables (Dutch, English or either).

## Themes

| Theme | Challenge as the organiser puts it |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Sport & Tech** (with the IOC) | "How can we use technology to make sports more accessible, fun, or safe?" Samsung NL page: "meer inclusie, beweging en verbinding" |
| **Sustainability & Tech** | Technology for the circular economy, nature conservation, animal protection, waste reduction or plastic pollution. Samsung NL page: "voor een leefbare toekomst" |

- The competition is phased: "the best teams progress to the next round" (Newsroom). **Open:** after which deliverable teams are cut.

## Prize and support

- **€2,500** for the winning team, meant to realise the project.
- Design Thinking workshop, personal coaching by Samsung experts, masterclasses from partners Soapbox and SkillsGym.
- **Open:** judging criteria. None of the three sources publish them.

## Past winner

- The previous Benelux edition was won by a smart bicycle helmet that warns the rider by vibration about blind-spot danger (Newsroom).
- What that suggests (an inference, not a stated rule): the jury has rewarded a tangible, demonstrable hardware prototype. It also means another safety wearable for cyclists will look like a repeat.
